The problem

The shop shows you a total. It hides the split.

You ask the price of a bangle. The reply is a single figure — say ₹1,08,000. Whether that's fair depends entirely on numbers the shop didn't break out. Most online “gold calculators” make it worse: they fetch a live rate you can't check against the board in front of you, or they quietly fold making charges into the metal price.

The one number you get ₹1,08,000 … built from what?

The headline

Now colour it: which rupees can actually move?

Metal value and GST follow the rate and the law — they're fixed. Wastage and making charges are shop policy — they're negotiable. goldmath puts that one figure at the top of every quote.

The honest number

It uses the stamp, not the fraction.

916

Under BIS standard IS 1417, hallmarked 22-carat gold is stamped 916 — a certified minimum of 91.6% purity. The tempting shortcut is 22 ÷ 24 = 91.667%. goldmath deliberately uses the stamped 916/1000, so the metal value is a slightly conservative, honest estimate rather than a rosy one. Every purity grade — 14K585 up to 24K995 — is cited to its source with the date it was verified.
